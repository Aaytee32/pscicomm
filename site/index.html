Ischemic Stroke: What Happens in the First 24 Hours

CBF, OEF & the Penumbra

By 24 hours, blood flow at the center of an ischemic stroke drops below 20% of normal. Here's how it gets there, step by step.

In this video
  • How the brain compensates first: arteriolar dilation, rising OEF, CBV and MTT (Fig. 3.7)
  • Core vs rim: why flow falls most in the central territory and collaterals protect the edges
  • The timeline: CBF, CMRO₂ and OEF from 1 hour to 24 hours and beyond
  • The rim of high oxygen extraction, lasting up to 16 hours, and its variable fate
  • Spontaneous recanalization, luxury perfusion, and the chronic infarct

For medical and pharmacy education: neurology, neuropharmacology and USMLE / NAPLEX / PLAB revision.

Source: Stroke: Pathophysiology, Diagnosis, and Management, 7th ed. (Grotta et al., Elsevier), pp. 30–31, paraphrased. Graphs are schematic, after Fig. 3.7.
